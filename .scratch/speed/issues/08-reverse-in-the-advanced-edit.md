# 08: Reverse in the Advanced Edit, and a reversed video Overlay

**Parent spec:** `spec.md` in this feature folder (user stories 17–19, 22; Solution "Reverse", "The Timeline", "Editing"; Implementation Decisions "The Advanced Edit"); ADR 0009.

**What to build:** on the desktop, the owner reverses a Segment or a video Overlay with the Reverse switch (or R) and edits it truly on the Timeline.

- **The Reverse switch** in the Speed section, with the copy's progress. R toggles it on the item picked alone. One step of undo.
- **A reversed video Overlay:** `reverse` on the Overlay (refused on an image), its copy of `offset .. offset + (end − start) × speed`, rendered and previewed as a reversed media Segment is (06), its sound reversed with it.
- **Trim:** on a reversed item the left edge moves `end` and the right edge `start`.
- **Split** of a reversed Segment: the left half on the Timeline is the later part of the range; both keep `reverse`; no new copy is made.
- **Filmstrip and Waveform** are counted from `end` and mirrored. **A badge** ◀ (with the Speed if not 1).
- **The Clipboard** carries `reverse`.

**Blocked by:** 04, 05, 06, 07.

**Status:** done

- [x] Toggling Reverse on a Segment shows the progress in the section and on the Segment, then plays it backwards; undo turns it off.
- [x] Dragging the left edge of a reversed Segment changes its range's end; the Source end snaps to Words on save.
- [x] Splitting a reversed Segment gives two halves whose joined playback equals the whole's, and no new copy is requested.
- [x] A reversed video Overlay renders and previews backwards with its sound.
- [x] The Filmstrip of a reversed Segment shows its last frame at its left edge.
- [x] R with nothing picked or with several items picked does nothing.

## Comments

**2026-10-17: done.**

**What was built:**
- **The model (backend):** `Overlay.reverse` (false by default), refused on an image by `_check_media_of` ("Overlay i is an image: it has no Speed, pitch or Reverse"; the MCP goes through the same check). `OverlayEvent` and `AudioEvent` carry `reverse`; `_overlay_event` sets it and `overlay_sound` passes it to the Overlay's sound.
- **Decided: `offset` stays the in-point of the forward range.** A reversed Overlay shows `offset .. offset + secs × speed` of its video backwards, so its left edge shows `offset + secs × speed` and its right edge (its last frame) `offset`. The Inspector's offset slider reads "Ends in the video at" in Reverse. The plan's event follows the same rule for what it shows: an Overlay cut by the Clip's end keeps the end of its part, so the event's `offset` begins later (`offset + (whole − end) × speed`); one cut by its video's end is cut as before. `copies_used` (the sweep) cuts an Overlay's range the same way, now also by the Clip's length, forwards too, so the sweep keeps exactly the copy the preview asked for.
- **Render:** `read_from_copies` rewrites reversed Overlay events and their sound events as it does Segments: `media` is the full-size copy's path (in `files`), `offset` is `copy.read(...)`'s start, `reverse=False`. The renderer, `_feeds` (a keyed Overlay's frame process) and `_audio_mix` are unchanged. `rendering._render` counts reversed Overlays and their sounds among the copies it makes as a render step (one copy serves the picture and the sound, and any narrower Overlay of the same video).
- **MCP:** `reverse` flows through the edit tools from the model. The tool list is 61 279 bytes, within the 61 500 budget. The Overlay paragraph of the instructions says "with `reverse`, that backwards, as a Segment's". The dry run's Overlay entry gives `reverse` and `copy` (`ready` / `made by the render`).
- **Preview:** `eventCopy`/`eventPlay` take `reverse`, so `PreviewOverlays` (the muted picture) and `PreviewMusic` (its sound) play the reversed copy at the Overlay's Speed, holding the frame before its part's end until it is made. `retimedCopy.ts` gains `servedBy`: a copy already made of a wider range of the same item, retimed alike, serves a narrower ask at once (its `at` moved, from the end in Reverse), so a split or a trim inward doesn't ask the backend again (`useRetimedCopies` consults it before asking).
- **The Advanced Edit:**
  - `actions.ts`: `setReverse(scene, item, reverse)`, one change. It keeps the range, the place and length on the Timeline, and the Keyframes. `reversible(scene, video)` returns the one picked Segment or video Overlay, or null for none, several, a Freeze frame, an image, a Text Layer or a Music Clip.
  - `SpeedFields` has the Reverse switch (with an `R` key cap) as one more row after the pitch switch. While the preview's copy is made, it shows that copy's progress: "Reversing… n%" or "Retiming… n%" (also for a Speed outside 0.25–4×), or "Couldn't reverse this video/Segment". The ask comes from the plan's Segment or Overlay event (`AdvancedEdit`'s `copyOf`, through `Inspector`), so the inspector polls the same copy the preview plays. The switch is in both `SegmentFields` (not on a Freeze frame, which shows its Duration) and `OverlayFields` (videos).
  - The keymap has a row `reverse`, R, in the Editing group of the sheet; `commands.ts` `more.reverse`, false with no reversible pick.
- **Trim:** in `draggedSegments`, a reversed Segment's left edge moves `end`, and its right edge moves `start`. The clamps are swapped to match: at least 0.3 s on the Timeline, within its file, and the longest clip for the Source. The Source end snaps to Words on save as a forward Segment's does (backend test). `retimed` re-anchors a reversed video's Keyframes from where its left edge now shows. A reversed Overlay's trim (`Tracks.tsx`) keeps its offset at the left edge and moves it at the right edge (`edits.keptOffset`). `TimelineItem` gains `trimMax`, the latest the end can be trimmed to: `maxSecs` limited both edges by one length, which can't express the reverse limits. Forwards, `trimMin`/`trimMax` now give the exact reach of the video (the old `maxSecs` could stop a forward start trim short). `TimingFields`' "Stays for" on a reversed Overlay moves its offset (its end in the video stays), within the video's start.
- **Split** of a reversed Segment: the first half on the Timeline is `{cut, end as played}`, the second `{start, cut}`. Both keep `speed`, `pitch` and `reverse`, and Keyframes are re-anchored; the whole's copy serves both (`servedBy`). An Overlay split, ripple-cut or pasted cut short in Reverse moves its offset by what is cut off its end, not its start (`keptOffset` in `actions.ts` KINDS, `ripple.ts`, `clipboard.ts`). The Clipboard carries `reverse` with the items.
- **Timeline:** `playingFrom(from, speed, reverse)` reads media backwards. `filmstripTiles`, `chunksAcross`, `filmstripLayer` and `waveformLayer` take `reverse` and are fed from the range's end (`paceAlong` in `Tracks.tsx`), so the Filmstrip and Waveform are mirrored. `PaceBadge` replaces 05's `SpeedBadge`: "2×", "◀" or "◀ 2×", beside 11's ❚❚ badge. Accessible names say ", reversed at 2×". A Source Segment's corner label shows the time at its left edge, and the inspector's range hint reads "0:10 back to 0:00 of the video".

**Decided differently, or for later:**
- **Keyframes stay where they are when Reverse is switched** (seconds from the item's start). A zoom-in stays a zoom-in in time, on other frames of the video. A Speed change scales them to keep the frames (05); Reverse doesn't mirror them. Revisit if the owner expects the motion to stay on the same frames.
- **"Progress on the Segment"** is the preview's (06/07's "Reversing… n%" inside the Segment's box, now also an Overlay's at the top of the frame, 09) and the Speed section's. The Timeline's Segment item shows no progress: it would mean a poll per item.
- **The section's progress appears once the plan has the item reversed** (the preview plan comes back after the edit). Until then it shows nothing.
- **Not checked in the browser** (no Job with a running backend in this worktree). The UI is typechecked and its actions tested.
- **12 (phone):** the inspector sheet already shows the Reverse switch and its progress (same `SpeedFields`). A phone Speed sheet can reuse `SpeedFields` with `copy` from `AdvancedEdit`'s `copyOf`, and `setItemReverse`.

**Review fixes (2026-10-17): Reverse mirrors Keyframes** (the owner decided, overturning "Keyframes stay where they are" above). `setReverse` maps every Keyframe of the Segment or video Overlay `at → length − at` (`keyActions.mirroredKeys`; the Segment's Timeline length, the Overlay's `secs`) when the flag actually changes, so the motion stays on the same frames of the video, as a Speed change scales them. Each span's Ease is reversed (`reversedEase`: `[x1, y1, x2, y2] → [1−x2, 1−y2, 1−x1, 1−y1]`, so Ease in becomes Ease out) and carried by the Keyframe that now ends that span; the new first Keyframe takes the old first one's unused Ease, so switching back gives the Keyframes exactly as they were. A Manual Scene's override Keyframes are not touched (they follow the Source; 07 plays them backwards). `reverse.test.ts`: values sampled at mirrored times match (a 2× video Segment with Linear, preset, custom and overshooting Eases, and an Overlay), the Eases in time order, the round trip, the same flag set twice, a Manual override untouched. The spec (Reverse), ADR 0009 (the Reverse bullet) and the glossary's Keyframe entry say so. The MCP doesn't mirror: an agent switching `reverse` sends the Keyframes it wants (as for Speed, ADR 0008).
